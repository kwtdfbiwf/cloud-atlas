OPTIMISED FOR
- Desktop Chrome with WebGL2, 1440 × 900 and larger.
- Responsive phone layout (portrait and short landscape), checked in browser emulation.
  Not yet tested on a physical iPhone or Android phone.

WHAT THE SITE DOES
- index.html: one page you scroll UP, from the ground (0 km) through the low, middle and high
  cloud layers to the summit (12.5 km). Floating titles hold the page at each change in the air
  (lifting off, the freezing level, into the ice).
- Each layer has chips (tabs) for its clouds and one card: tip, live "From the ground" view,
  typical base, made of, rain, weather. "More about …" opens a dialog with how to spot it, how it
  forms and a fact. On phones the card docks at the bottom and opens as a bottom sheet with
  everything in it.
- "Your sky now" (dialog) → sky.html: choose a place (location or search) and a time; the page
  climbs through only the clouds estimated there, with Open-Meteo weather (CC BY 4.0).
- Night sky toggle, optional sound (wind, rain, thunder), a movable sun, altitude jumps.

HOW IT'S BUILT
- Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. three.js r160 draws a Draco-compressed cloud model
  (reference-clouds/reference-clouds.glb, about 0.8 MB) built in Blender.
- Fallbacks: ten bundled cloud photographs replace the 3D sky when WebGL2 is unavailable,
  the context is lost or the renderer fails to load, or with ?lightweight / ?no-webgl2
  (a notice explains why). ?motion=reduce, or the system setting, gives a still sky with no
  camera turns, rain or decorative movement.
- Accessibility: reading order is ground → summit (the climb is reversed visually with CSS),
  skip link, offscreen controls removed from Tab order, chips as tabs with arrow keys, phone
  sheets trap focus and close with ×, a tap outside, swipe down or Escape.

RETIRED (kept in ../cloud-atlas-archive and in git history)
- The raymarched volumetric renderer and its noise worker; the clay-sky, prepared-sky and
  cumulus-study experiments; the Field tools (compare cloud sizes, identification quiz).

Learning content: the cards in clouds.js. Height ranges overlap; the altimeter shows your
viewpoint, not a cloud's base. Cloud estimates on the sky page come from forecast data, not a camera.
